Article: Cognitive bias
- A cognitive bias is a systematic pattern of deviation from norm or rationality in judgment.
- Individuals create their own "subjective reality" from their perception of the input.
- An individual's construction of reality, not the objective input, may dictate their behavior in the world.
- Thus, cognitive biases may sometimes lead to perceptual distortion, inaccurate judgment, illogical interpretation, or what is broadly called irrationality.
- Although reality might be a stimulus, it is the individual's constructed reality that affects their behavior, not the actual reality.
- Cognitive biases are often a result of the brain's attempt to simplify information processing.
- They arise from various processes that are sometimes difficult to distinguish.
- Cognitive biases can be distinguished from logical fallacies.
